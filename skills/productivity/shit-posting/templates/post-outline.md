# Substack / X / LinkedIn Outline (Patrick's Voice)

Fill each section. The phrasing suggestions are in your voice — tweak liberally, but keep the structure.

**Format is named by Patrick, never inferred.** Notes, Articles, X posts and LinkedIn posts are different mediums, not different lengths. Caps are ceilings, not targets: **Note ≤ 200 characters**, **Article ≤ 800 words**, **X post ≤ 280 characters per post** (links cost 23 characters each, whatever their length), **LinkedIn post ≤ 3,000 characters** (LinkedIn's own limit — the only cap here that comes from a platform rather than from your taste).

> FORMAT: note | article | post | linkedin

> TYPE: thread   (posts only, optional)

# TYPE N — Note (≤ 200 characters)

One idea. No title, no alternate title, no headers, no tag block, no takeaway list, no "To be clear:" — nothing labeled. The cap covers the whole body including any link URL (a pasted Substack URL eats 50-60 characters).

## The line

> [The whole point in one sentence, front-loaded — the feed truncates. One idea, said once.]

## Optional second line

> [The turn, the caveat, or the dry aside. At this length it's the wit line OR the detail, never both.]

## Optional link or image

> [A link gets a rich preview; linking your own post is a legitimate use. Budget the characters.]

# TYPE B — Article: News-cycle commentary

For a piece riding a news event (a CEO quote, a layoff, a product launch). Lead with the news, then the analysis. The chip salesman post is the model. *Can use the full 800-word cap.*

## Title

[Headline that names the news and the angle. "Breaking News; ..." works when the news is genuinely fresh.]

### Alternate Title: [The contrast or the framing — "One month, two talk-tracks: to the market X, to his own staff Y."]

---

## Lead with the news

> [The event, first. Who, what, when, with a source link. No throat-clearing. "Qualcomm told employees Monday it will cut jobs worldwide..."]

---

## The numbers / the evidence

> [The quarter, the filing, the data that undercuts or explains the news. Cite the primary source (10-Q, earnings, filing) not just the press writeup. "Then read the quarter it lands on..."]

---

## The contrast (the pivot)

> [The same actor saying something different to a different audience, or the gap between the story and the reality. "Less than two weeks before the memo, the same CEO was telling the market..."]

---

## The technical meat

> [The analysis that proves you can hold the engineering conversation: the memory math, the spec sheet, the architecture. Link everything.]

---

## To be clear

> [Defuse the alarmism without softening the real claim. Name who's actually affected and who isn't.]

---

## The quirk / the tell

> [One detail that reveals the real motive — the unnamed customers, the internal contradiction, the timing. "The timing is the tell..."]

---

## The broader reminder

> [The general principle the news illustrates: "executives narrate the future that justifies their revenue." One sharp line.]

---

## Takeaways / questions to ask (optional)

- [ ] Ask which companies. No names, no commitment.
- [ ] Do the memory math yourself: parameters times bytes per parameter.
- [ ] Read the vendor's own spec sheet.
- [ ] When layoffs come framed as "performance," read the quarter.

---

## Sign-off

> [Brief, human, varied. A quiet line that lands the thesis, or a note to the people affected. No subscribe CTA.]

# TYPE X — Post (≤ 280 characters)

One idea, same discipline as a Note with tighter physics: public timeline, strangers in the replies, a quote-post preview that truncates well before the cap. A standard 280 account — no Premium long-form. Links cost exactly 23 characters.

## The line

> [The point, front-loaded. It has to survive a quote-post preview, so land it inside the first ~120 characters.]

## Optional turn

> [The aside, the caveat, the counterpunch. One, maximum.]

## Optional link

> [Budget 23 characters, not the URL's real length. Take the rich preview.]

## Hashtags (optional)

> [0-2, inline, only when they route the post to a real audience. Never a tag block.]

# TYPE X — Thread (each post ≤ 280 characters)

Unnumbered. Every post independently readable, because threads get picked up from the middle. The link goes in the **last** post. Separate the posts with a line containing only `===` — a file convention, never pasted.

===

## Post 2 — the mechanism

> [The meat. Still standalone.]

===

## Post 3 — the payoff

> [The landing, carrying the link. 23 characters of budget.]

# TYPE L — LinkedIn post (≤ 3,000 characters)

Read at two speeds: ~140 characters by most of the feed, the whole thing by the few who tap "...see more". Draft the hook first, then the body. **Plain text only** — no markdown, no `**bold**`, no unicode-bold; line breaks and short paragraphs are the formatting. One ask per post. No tag block unless the tags route somewhere real.

Subtypes: `technical` (a finding, a PSA), `commentary` (riding news), `job-search` (an availability or referral ask), `company` (a Peregrine Design Werx post).

## The hook (~140 characters)

> [The claim, the ask, or the number. This is the only line most readers get, so no greeting, no "I'm excited to share", no hashtag. 140 characters, hard.]

## The context

> [One short paragraph: what happened, what he's after, what changed. Keep it to two or three lines.]

## The substance

> [The specifics — role, industry, stage, tools, numbers. Vague posts get vague help. Concrete beats clever.]

## The ask or the takeaway

> [Exactly one. "Put us in touch" / "here's what I'd check on your own machine" / "the trade is worth naming". If you're stacking two asks, you're writing two posts.]

## Optional close

> [A plain question, an open door to DMs, or one quiet line. Never "thoughts?", never "repost for reach".]

## Cap check

```bash
python3 ~/.hermes/skills/productivity/shit-posting/scripts/count.py linkedin <draft.md>
```

Reports the count against LinkedIn's 3,000, plus the fold positions, first-paragraph length, external-link count and hashtag count — advisory only, they never fail the draft.

## TYPE L — LinkedIn `job-search` post

The one LinkedIn type where the ask is not optional. Five things, in order:

1. **Available, in the first line.** Buried availability posts fail. Don't bury it behind a scene-setter.
2. **Title, industry, location.** One role, one place. "Account Executive, AI/ML infrastructure, remote or Vermont" — not "open to opportunities in tech".
3. **One or two proofs with a detail.** Not a résumé: the résumé is the profile this post sends people to. A number does the work an adjective can't.
4. **One specific ask.** Introductions or referrals, stated plainly. "Introductions welcome" beats "any leads?"; "open to anything" is not an ask.
5. **Optional close.** DMs open, or a plain question.

Pair the post with the Open to Work setting if he wants that signal visible — the post and the setting should tell the same story, and the setting has its own audience control (recruiters only, or everyone).

**Pitfall:** the layoff beat is used once. If a layoff post has already gone out recently, a job-search post that re-tells it reads as circling — lead with what's next instead.

---

**Type L pitfalls:** 3,000 characters is room, not a target — the fold and the one-ask rule are what decide whether it works. Don't write "thrilled to announce" or "excited to share"; that's not his register. And never state a LinkedIn reach claim (link penalty, golden hour, editing) as fact — see the documented-vs-repeated table in the skill.
