# Compliance and Risk

**Read this before buying anything you intend to resell.** The purpose of this
file is to make the constraint explicit so the decision is a deliberate one, not
an accident discovered when an account gets suspended.

## ExpertVoice — the honor code

Source: https://www.expertvoice.com/honor-code-2/

ExpertVoice is an industry-insider pricing platform open to people who qualify by
occupation (retail employees, healthcare professionals, military and veterans,
fitness instructors, first responders, and similar). The honor code is
written as a set of personal-conduct rules, not a resale contract, and its
clauses are about **personal use and discretion**:

- **No discussing your membership or the discounts.** Access is unique to the
  member and is not to be discussed with others — including not telling a retail
  store, not telling a friend, and not asking a store to match the pricing.
- **No shopping for others.** "Your one-of-a-kind brand access is just for you,
  based on who you are and what you've achieved as an expert." It directs members
  to check each brand's own rules and guidelines before purchasing.
- **No price-matching requests.** The pricing is exclusive access, not a coupon
  or a public sale.
- **Enforcement is stated explicitly:** "We monitor accounts closely and cancel
  those used unwisely."

### What that means operationally

- **The brand's rules page is the binding document, not the honor code.** Brand
  and per-drop terms can and do prohibit resale, transfer, or commercial use.
  Terms on brand-side pages have been documented prohibiting providing or
  accepting compensation or consideration in connection with a discount code,
  with forfeiture of the benefit as the stated remedy. Read the specific brand's
  page at the moment you buy, and keep a copy.
- **Discount codes are single-use and time-boxed.** Brand guidance on
  ExpertVoice has described codes as voided once used, non-duplicable, and valid
  for a limited window before refresh. Treat a code as a one-shot authorization.
- **Documented enforcement path:** misuse — including sharing or reselling codes
  — is described by third-party guides as resulting in order cancellations and
  potential suspension from the platform.
- **Never mention ExpertVoice in a listing, DM, or negotiation.** Even where the
  item's resale is unproblematic, referencing the source of the discount
  violates the discretion clause directly and is the fastest route to losing the
  account.

### Decision rule

> Open the brand's rules page. If it prohibits resale, transfer, or commercial
> use — **that item is buy-for-self only.** Stop. Do not "test" it with one
> listing. The asymmetry is brutal: a few dollars of margin against losing the
> entire membership and every other brand attached to it.

If the rules page is silent, you are in a judgement call, not a clearance. Default
to personal use for high-visibility branded gear (anything the brand actively
prices, anything with an active MAP program, anything that competes with their
retail channel). The lower-signal the item, the lower the account risk.

## Nextdoor — reseller listings are prohibited outright

Source: https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/Best-practices-For-Sale-Free

This is the cleanest rule of the three channels and it removes Nextdoor from the
flip plan. Explicitly **not allowed** in Nextdoor's For Sale and Free section:

- "Listing items that you are selling for a business, **including resellers**, and
  commercial consignment and estate sellers"
- Listing items as part of an incentive sales program (e.g. Mary Kay, Amway,
  Avon, Scentsy)
- Posting links to items on other classified sites
- Listing the same item or service more than once at the same time
- Deleting and reposting a listing to increase visibility (reposting is allowed
  once the prior listing expires)
- Overposting listings in a way that blocks neighbors' fair visibility
- Gift cards; prohibited goods and services; price gouging

The Seller Code of Conduct adds: **"Don't profit from the generosity of other
neighbors"** — reselling items received free on Nextdoor is a stated breach of
trust.

Stated enforcement: a warning, temporary or permanent read-only access, or
temporary or permanent account suspension. Nextdoor notes it may require
complaints from multiple buyers before escalating, since off-platform disputes
can't be verified.

### What Nextdoor IS good for

- **Sourcing.** Buy the neighbor's unused gear cheaply, then list it on eBay or
  FB Marketplace. The restriction is on *selling as a reseller*, not on buying.
- **Personal-item liquidation.** Your own used household goods, one at a time,
  are exactly what the category is for.
- **Local pickup logistics** for genuine personal sales with no purchase protection.

Keep the resale inventory off Nextdoor entirely. A flagged reseller post costs
the account, and the account is tied to your real home address.

## MAP pricing and VeRO takedowns

- **MAP (minimum advertised price)** is a contract term between a brand and its
  authorized dealers — generally **not enforceable by a brand against an
  unauthorized reseller**, and eBay's VeRO program is not a mechanism for MAP
  enforcement. That's the legal position; it is not protection from practical
  consequences.
- **Practical consequence:** brands track listings regardless of authorization
  and can send cease-and-desist letters or pursue listing removal. Under-pricing
  a brand's product visibly is what attracts the attention.
- **VeRO (Verified Rights Owner) takedowns** target intellectual-property
  violations: using the brand's marketing photography, logos, or trademarked
  copy in your listing. This is the takedown risk you actually control.
- **Mitigation:** shoot your own photos of the actual item, write your own copy,
  use the brand name descriptively only ("Brand Model X jacket, size L, new with
  tags"), never paste marketing text or stock imagery, and price at or above the
  going retail street price rather than below it.

## Account-suspension paths, ranked by likelihood

1. **ExpertVoice account loss** — for sharing/reselling codes or visible
   discretion breaches. Highest impact, because it takes the supply away.
2. **Nextdoor suspension** — for a reseller listing. Fast, reported by neighbors.
3. **eBay VeRO listing removal** — for brand imagery/copy. Recoverable, but
   repeated strikes risk the account.
4. **eBay seller defects** — item-not-as-described, late shipment, cancellation.
   Kills Top Rated status and raises FVF-adjacent costs.

## Tax and record-keeping

- eBay and Meta are TPSOs; 1099-K at $20,000 / 200 transactions (federal).
- Below the threshold, business income is still income. Keep every ExpertVoice
  order confirmation — it is your cost basis and the only proof of what you paid.
- Log each flip the day it sells: SKU, cost, channel, list price, sold price, net,
  days-to-sell. Without the cost column, a year of flipping looks like pure profit
  to the IRS.

## The honest summary

Two of the three channels you named are clean: **eBay and Facebook Marketplace**.
**Nextdoor is not available for resale inventory** — it is a sourcing channel and
nothing more. And the supply side carries account risk that varies entirely by
brand rules, so the compliance gate in step 1 of the procedure is doing real work,
not ceremony.
