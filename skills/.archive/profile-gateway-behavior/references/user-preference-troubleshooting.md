# User Preference Alignment in Troubleshooting

## Key Insights

The user has a clear preference for direct, actionable solutions over meta-commentary:

1. **"For once and for all" solutions** - They want comprehensive fixes that address the root cause, not incremental steps
2. **Direct commands** - They prefer specific executable commands over explanations of why those steps are needed
3. **Minimal explanation** - They dislike verbosity and overly reactive responses, preferring concise prose over bullet lists
4. **Actionable output** - They want to see what actually works, not process documentation

## Application to Hermes Troubleshooting

In the gateway behavior case, they explicitly said they prefer "for once and for all" solutions over incremental fixes. This means when they see a behavior that seems buggy, they want to understand why it's actually working as designed rather than being treated as a regression.

## Example from Conversation

In the profile switching scenario:
- They were confused by "disconnected gateway" warnings when switching to new profiles
- Their concern was that this was a new behavior rather than expected behavior
- They wanted to understand why it was working as designed, not be told it's a bug

## Pattern Recognition

When troubleshooting with this user:
1. **Start with the concrete solution** rather than process explanation
2. **Use direct commands** that they can execute immediately
3. **Align explanations with their preference** for minimal, actionable content
4. **Show the "why" only when explicitly requested** or when it's essential to understanding the solution
