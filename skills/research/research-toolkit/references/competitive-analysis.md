# Competitive Analysis

This skill provides a structured approach to conducting competitive analysis for products, services, or companies. It covers gathering information, comparing features, pricing, positioning, and synthesizing insights into actionable reports.

## When to Use This Skill

- Evaluating market positioning of a product or service
- Preparing for product launches, go-to-market strategies, or fundraising
- Benchmarking against competitors for product development
- Creating sales enablement materials (battle cards, comparison sheets)
- Supporting partnership or acquisition decisions

## Key Steps

### 1. Define Scope and Objectives
- Clearly state the purpose of the analysis (e.g., feature gap, pricing strategy, go-to-market).
- Identify the target competitors (direct, indirect, emerging).
- Determine the dimensions to compare (features, pricing, technology, market share, customer perception, etc.).

### 2. Gather Data
- **Primary sources**: competitor websites, product pages, pricing sheets, press releases, investor reports.
- **Secondary sources**: industry reports, analyst firm publications, news articles, social media, customer reviews.
- **Tools**: web search, web extraction, documentation scraping, financial databases.
- **Tip**: Use structured queries (e.g., site:competitor.com "pricing") and capture URLs for citation.

### 3. Organize Information
- Create a comparison matrix (competitors vs. features/attributes).
- Use consistent scoring or weighting if quantitative comparison is needed.
- Capture qualitative notes (e.g., unique selling points, customer sentiment).

### 4. Analyze and Synthesize
- Identify patterns: where competitors are strong/weak, gaps in the market.
- Look for pricing models, bundling strategies, and go-to-market tactics.
- Summarize findings in a clear narrative: executive summary, detailed comparison, strategic implications.

### 5. Deliver Insights
- Tailor the output to the audience (executive summary for leadership, detailed matrix for product team).
- Include actionable recommendations (e.g., feature prioritization, pricing adjustments, differentiation messaging).
- Cite sources transparently to build credibility.

## Best Practices

- **Stay objective**: rely on verifiable data; avoid speculation.
- **Keep it up-to-date**: competitive landscapes change rapidly; note the date of information.
- **Leverage multiple sources**: triangulate information to improve accuracy.
- **Visualize effectively**: use tables, charts, and heatmaps to make comparisons digestible.
- **Focus on decisions**: highlight insights that directly inform strategy or tactics.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Scope creep** – trying to compare too many competitors or too many attributes leads to analysis paralysis.
2. **Outdated information** – especially for pricing and feature sets; verify freshness.
3. **Overlooking indirect competitors** – sometimes the biggest threat comes from adjacent markets.
4. **Ignoring context** – a feature may be present but poorly implemented; note usability where possible.
5. **Presenting raw data without interpretation** – always connect findings to implications for your own product/business.
